# Kegelee video ads for Instagram and Facebook

Four finished video ads in the app's own look (black and white, Archivo, the
KE logo and the training circle), plus scripts for more ads you can film on a
phone with free tools.

## The four ads

| # | File | Length | Hook | Why it works |
|---|------|--------|------|--------------|
| 1 | `videos/01-invisible-*` | 16s | "I'm working out right now." | A deadpan joke with a payoff ("and nobody can tell"). Gets shared and commented on. |
| 2 | `videos/02-trythis-*` | 19s | "Try this right now." | People do a Kegel along with the circle, so they watch to the end. Long watch time lowers your cost per result. |
| 3 | `videos/03-legday-*` | 16s | Chest day ✓ Back day ✓ … Pelvic floor ✗ | Gym-split joke for a male fitness audience. It's white, so it stands out among dark ads in a feed. |
| 4 | `videos/04-screentime-*` | 16s | "Screen time today: 4h 12m" | A comparison that's hard to argue with. It also shows the most app features. |

Each ad comes in three files:

- `*-9x16.mp4`: 1080×1920 for Reels and Stories. This is the main file to upload.
- `*-4x5.mp4`: 1080×1350 for the Instagram and Facebook feeds. It's a crop of the same video.
- `*-cover.jpg`: the cover or thumbnail frame.

All text sits inside the area that Reels/Stories buttons and the 4:5 feed crop
don't cover. You can upload the files as they are.

The audio is a short track of sound effects: thumps on each word, ticks on the
countdown, a whoosh and a chime. Put music on top of it (see below).

## Adding music for free

Popular Instagram songs are **not licensed for ads**. Ads that use them get
muted or rejected. Use one of these instead:

- **Meta Sound Collection** (free and cleared for ads): Meta Business Suite →
  Content → Sound Collection. You can also pick a track in Ads Manager when you
  build the ad.
- **CapCut** (free): import the MP4, then Audio → Sounds. Use only tracks
  marked for commercial use. Keep the music around 30–40% volume so the
  thumps still come through.

Upbeat phonk, minimal techno or a punchy hip-hop beat at 100–130 BPM suits
ads 1, 3 and 4. For ad 2, use a calm beat: people are breathing along with it.

## Uploading in Meta Ads Manager

1. **Fix tracking first.** The app has no Meta SDK and no attribution tool
   (AppsFlyer, Adjust and so on). Without one, Meta can't see installs or
   trial starts, so it can't find people likely to install. Until one is
   added, run a **Traffic** campaign that sends people to the Play Store or
   App Store listing. Once the SDK is in, switch to an **App promotion**
   campaign that optimizes for installs, or better, for trial starts.
2. Create one ad set. Choose ages 18+, Advantage+ audience and Advantage+
   placements.
3. Create one ad per video. In the ad, use **"Customize media per placement"**:
   upload the 9×16 file for Stories and Reels and the 4×5 file for Feeds.
4. Paste the copy for that ad (below). Set the button to **Install now** (or
   **Download**).
5. Turn off Meta's automatic "enhancements" that add music or change the
   text. They often clash with the ads' design.

## Staying within Meta's ad rules

Kegel ads get rejected for three reasons. Avoid all three and your ads stay
live:

1. **No sex-performance claims.** "Last longer in bed", "stronger in bed" and
   "better sex" break Meta's adult-content rules. The app's first onboarding
   slide says "Stronger in bed". That's fine inside the app but not in an ad.
   Words like "control", "core" and "confidence" are safe.
2. **Don't say what the viewer has.** "Do you leak when you sneeze?" or
   "Struggling with bladder control?" breaks the personal-attributes rule.
   Say "I" (a creator talking about themselves) or speak generally ("the
   muscle everyone skips").
3. **No medical promises.** Don't say "cures", "fixes", "treats" or "doctor
   recommended". The app is a fitness and education tool.

Set the age to 18+. All four ads above follow these rules.

## More ads to film yourself (free, phone + CapCut)

Ads filmed by real people on a phone (called UGC, "user-generated content")
usually get more installs than polished animation. Film with the front
camera, upright, in natural light. Add captions with CapCut → Text → Auto
captions, using a bold white font with a black outline. For the app shots,
record your own phone screen during a real session. Add an end screen by
cutting in the last 3 seconds of any ad above.

**A. "The meeting" (no talking, about 10s)**
- Shot: you on a video call or at a desk, staring at the camera, face
  completely still. Squeeze subtly.
- On-screen text: `me doing kegels in the meeting` → (3s) `nobody knows` →
  cut to a screen recording of the app circle → end screen.

**B. "Guess what I'm doing" (two people, about 12s)**
- A: "Guess what I'm doing right now." B (stares): "...nothing?" A: "Exactly."
- Cut to the app circle. Text: `1 minute a day. Nobody can tell.` → end screen.

**C. Green-screen explainer (talking, about 20s)**
- In CapCut, use Cutout → Remove background, and put the app screen recording
  behind you.
- Script: "This is the one muscle almost nobody trains: your pelvic floor.
  It supports your bladder and your core. Training it takes one minute. This
  app runs the session for you. Follow the circle, squeeze, hold, relax. It
  even measures how long you can hold, so you can see it go up. Link's
  right there."

**D. "Day 1 vs Day 30" (talking, about 15s)**
- Show your **real** hold measurement from the app's Progress tab on day 1 and
  again 30 days later. Never fake the numbers: made-up results break Meta's
  rules and people notice.
- Script: "Day one I could hold for 3 seconds. Day thirty…" (show the
  number) "…one minute a day, that's it."

**E. "Things I wish I knew sooner" (talking, about 20s)**
- Works for the women's market. Say it in the first person.
- Script: "Three things I wish I'd known about my pelvic floor sooner. One:
  it's a muscle, and it trains like one. Two: you're probably squeezing your
  butt instead. Belly, butt and thighs should stay relaxed. Three: it takes
  one minute a day. I use an app that counts it out for me."

## Testing and scaling

- Start with all four ads in one ad set at **$15–20 a day**. Let them run for
  3–4 days without changes so Meta can learn.
- Check two numbers in Ads Manager (Columns → Customize → Video):
  - **Hook rate** = 3-second video plays ÷ impressions. Aim for over 30%.
    If it's lower, the first second isn't working, so change the hook.
  - **Hold rate** = ThruPlays ÷ 3-second plays. Aim for over 15%. If it's
    lower, the middle of the ad is losing people.
- Turn off anything costing twice as much per result as your best ad. Make 2–3
  new versions of the winner: same structure, new hook or new first scene.
  Fresh ads stop audiences getting bored of the same video.
- Once you can measure trial starts, judge ads by **cost per trial**, not
  cost per install. A cheap install that never subscribes doesn't help.

## Changing the videos

The ads are built from `source/ads.html`: the text, timings and screens are all
in the `ADS` object there. To see one frame in a browser, open
`ads.html?ad=invisible&t=1.2` through any local web server. Add `&guides=1` to
show the safe zones. To render the videos again:

```sh
node marketing/ads/source/render.mjs            # all four
node marketing/ads/source/render.mjs legday     # just one
```

This needs Node with Playwright, ffmpeg, and python3 with numpy. The script
writes to `marketing/ads/videos/`.
